# Intake and updates

## Start with one useful outcome

| What Mel wants to do | Ask first | Reuse in |
|---|---|---|
| Start the newsletter | What do you want someone to get from the first email? Then check whether the signup form and welcome message are already running. | Content Planner, Newsletter, Audience Growth |
| Sound like herself | Which public post or approved draft feels most like you? Ask what feels wrong in a draft before asking her to define her brand voice. | Business Context, Newsletter, Social Content |
| Understand money and deadlines | What's the question or next bill? Ask for the period and relevant totals. For GST, explain it as a tax on sales and ask whether she is registered or already charging it. | Sole Trader Tax, Bookkeeping, Admin |
| Launch an offer | What could people pay for first and how much time can you give it? Confirm the audience and delivery details before drafting checkout. | Audience Growth, Newsletter, Online Sales |
| Handle records or a mistake | What kind of information and system is involved? Ask for categories and whether it is still exposed. | Practice Privacy |

Reuse existing answers instead of repeating the whole questionnaire. For private information ask for totals, dates, broad categories and redacted excerpts. Do not request tax identifiers, credentials, customer names, contact details, bank details, clinical records or subscriber exports. Keep detailed finance and clinical data in their source systems.

## Turn answers into shared context

Record each proposed item with the value, period or effective date, source and date heard, status, decision owner and affected skills. Distinguish public observation, Simon-reported, Mel-confirmed, proposed and open. Record 'not sure' as open. Keep previous dated values for changes such as monthly turnover. If the person who owns a fact clearly corrects it, use the new value with its source and effective date or period. Retain the old value as superseded. When authority, meaning or timing remains ambiguous and affects the task, show the difference and ask one focused question before relying on it. A statement by Mel can establish a fact she owns. It does not imply approval of a draft or permission to send it.

Use the existing Business Context profile for approved, non-sensitive practice facts and preferences. Keep private figures and operational details in a user-approved working record outside the repository, or use them for the current answer only. Confirm where the user wants durable information saved before moving it to a new destination. In later work, retrieve the approved record if available and say what was used. If it is unavailable, ask for only the missing fact needed for the task.

Distinguish business learning from skill changes. A correction to a price changes context. A recurring failure to distinguish fee and payout changes the bookkeeping instructions. For a workflow change, capture a synthetic reproduction and expected behaviour, revise the smallest relevant instruction, then rerun the original and a different scenario. Keep one-off preferences in context unless they describe a durable workflow choice.
